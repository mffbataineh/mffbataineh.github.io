    🔤 المفردات: المعنى والمرادف والضد

    Vocabulary: meanings, synonyms (مرادف) and opposites (ضد)

    Three words you must know for this section:
    • مُرادِف (muradif) = synonym, a word with the same meaning. Relationship: تَرادُف. e.g. كَلَل / مَلَل
    • ضِدّ (didd) = opposite. Relationship: تَضادّ. e.g. صَباح / مَساء
    • تَشْبيه (tashbih) = a comparison/simile ("like..."). e.g. the mountain is like the Mona Lisa.

    🧩 الجملة الاسمية وأنواع الخبر

    The nominal sentence and the 4 types of خبر

    Step 1: two kinds of sentence

    Look at the first word:
    • Starts with a verb (an action: يَمُرُّ، يَرْعى، يَتَرَبَّعُ) → جُمْلَة فِعْلِيَّة (verbal sentence)
    • Starts with a noun (a name/thing: الجَبَلُ، الإماراتُ) → جُمْلَة اسْمِيَّة (nominal sentence)
    A nominal sentence has two parts: the مُبْتَدَأ (the topic: who/what we talk about) and the خَبَر (the news: what we say about it). Think: Topic + News.
    جَبَلُ حَفِيتٍ
    مُبْتَدَأ · topic
    مَوْجُودٌ
    خَبَر · the news
    فِي كُلِّ شَارِعٍ
    extra detail

    "Jebel Hafeet is present in every street." The news about the mountain is that it is موجود, so the خبر is موجودٌ.

    ✏️ الهمزة المتوسطة على الألف والواو

    The middle hamza (ء): where does it sit?

    The rule: the strongest vowel wins 💪

    When a hamza is in the middle of a word, compare two vowels: the vowel on the hamza and the vowel on the letter before it. The stronger vowel chooses the seat:
    1. kasra ( ـِ , "i") is the strongest → sits on a nabra: ئ
    2. damma ( ـُ , "u") → sits on waw: ؤ
    3. fatha ( ـَ , "a") → sits on alif: أ
    4. sukun ( ـْ , no vowel) is the weakest
    Memory trick: i > u > a > silent. Your exam focuses on أ and ؤ.

    📝 الكتابة والتعبير الوصفي

    Descriptive writing: you'll get one of these two in the exam

    How to get full marks: correct spelling (سلامة اللغة والإملاء), describing words like adjectives and "like..." comparisons (التراكيب الوصفية), and punctuation (علامات الترقيم): ، . ! ؟ :
    Plan in 3 parts: Beginning (where/when) → Middle (what you saw, did, felt) → End (why you liked it / a wish).
