CONDENSED SPEAKER SCRIPT - IEEE AICT 2025
Offline Multimodal Edge AI Framework for ICT Accessibility Systems
================================================================

SLIDE 1: TITLE SLIDE (30 seconds)
-------------------
"Good morning. I'm Mohammad Al Bataineh from UAEU's Electrical Engineering Department. 

Today I'll present our offline multimodal edge AI framework for assistive technology - a privacy-first approach that runs entirely on Raspberry Pi 5. This is collaborative work with colleagues from Mechanical Engineering and Computer Science."


SLIDE 2: MOTIVATION & PROBLEM (1 minute)
---------------------------------------
"Current assistive systems rely on cloud processing, creating three critical problems:

First, privacy concerns - sensitive personal data transmitted externally.
Second, network dependency - requires constant internet.
Third, latency from network delays.

Our solution: 100% offline operation on edge hardware with sub-1.2 second latency, complete data sovereignty, and integrated vision, speech, and text processing."


SLIDE 3: KEY CONTRIBUTIONS (1.5 minutes)
--------------------------
"Our results show: 23-31% inference time reduction, 45% less redundant OCR processing, 9.8/10 privacy score, and zero crashes in 50-hour testing.

Four novel contributions:

One - Optimization strategies: queue-based inference, adaptive buffers, priority scheduling, intelligent caching.

Two - First concurrent execution of YOLOv8, Tesseract OCR, face recognition, and VOSK speech on Raspberry Pi 5.

Three - Complete privacy with zero external data transmission.

Four - Systematic evaluation across environmental awareness, text reading, and social interaction scenarios."


SLIDE 4: RELATED WORK (1 minute)
--------------------------------
"This table compares our work to existing systems.

Previous work: limited to 1-2 modalities, often cloud-hybrid approaches, privacy-performance trade-offs.

Our framework: 4+ modalities, full privacy at 9.8/10, latency 850-1240ms per modality.

We address three gaps: limited multimodal integration, insufficient optimization for embedded platforms, and privacy-performance compromises."


SLIDE 5: ARCHITECTURE (1.5 minutes)
----------------------------
"Our modular architecture has three input streams: camera, microphone, and user commands.

Three processing modules: Computer Vision with YOLOv8, Speech Processing with VOSK, and Natural Language Processing.

The Central Processing Coordinator manages task scheduling, resource allocation, and inter-module communication with priority-based management.

Outputs: audio feedback and system responses.

This modular design enables independent operation while the coordinator ensures efficient resource sharing."


SLIDE 6: HARDWARE & SOFTWARE (1 minute)
-------------------------------------------
"Hardware: Raspberry Pi 5 with ARM Cortex-A76 at 2.4GHz, 8GB RAM. Camera Module V3 for vision. USB microphone array. Total cost: under $200.

Software: YOLOv8 nano for object detection, Tesseract for OCR, OpenCV for faces, VOSK for speech-to-text, pyttsx3 for audio output.

Multi-threaded architecture maximizes CPU utilization while maintaining responsiveness."


SLIDE 7: OPTIMIZATIONS (2 minutes)
---------------------------------
"Four key optimizations:

One - Queue-based inference: 23% latency reduction from 1100ms to 850ms.

Two - Adaptive buffering: dynamic sizing from 10-50 commands, 90% crash reduction.

Three - Priority scheduling: audio Priority 1, face Priority 2, objects Priority 3, OCR Priority 4. Result: 60% faster voice response under load.

Four - Intelligent caching: reuse similar results, 45% less OCR processing.

These enable multimodal AI on $200 hardware."


SLIDE 8: METHODOLOGY (1 minute)
---------------------------------
"Testing across three lighting scenarios: well-lit, moderate, dim. Two audio conditions: quiet and noisy.

Metrics: end-to-end latency, precision, recall, resource utilization at 100ms intervals.

50 repetitions per condition. Statistical validation with paired t-tests.

Three scenarios: environmental awareness (target <1000ms), text reading (target <1500ms), social interaction (target <1000ms)."


SLIDE 9: RESULTS (2 minutes)
----------------------------
"Performance results:

Computer Vision: 850ms latency, 85.3% accuracy - target met.
Speech Recognition: 400-700ms, 88.7% accuracy - target met.
OCR: 1240ms, 91.4% accuracy - target met.
Face Recognition: 980ms, 78.9% accuracy - target met.
Integrated: 1680ms with 80-90% CPU usage.

Statistical validation: 28% latency reduction (p=0.003), 12% accuracy improvement (p=0.007), zero crashes in 50 hours.

These demonstrate practical viability for assistive applications."


SLIDE 10: RESOURCE UTILIZATION (1 minute)
---------------------------------------
"CPU usage: Vision 45%, Speech 29%, OCR 36%, Face 38%. Combined: 80-90%.

Memory: stable at 1.2GB baseline, 1.4GB peak. Zero overflow events.

Dynamic quantization: FP16 at low load (85.3% accuracy), INT8 at high load (81.2% accuracy). 4% accuracy trade-off maintains responsiveness.

Efficient multimodal execution on constrained hardware."


SLIDE 11: PRIVACY VS PERFORMANCE (1.5 minutes)
------------------------------------------------
"Privacy score formula: 0.4×Local Processing + 0.4×No Transmission + 0.2×User Control.

Our system: L=10 (fully local), N=10 (zero transmission), U=9 (full control) = 9.8/10.

Other edge systems: 7.2-8.6/10.
Cloud systems: 2-4/10 due to external transmission.

Our latency: 850-1680ms - competitive with edge, often faster than cloud.

Key insight: superior privacy without sacrificing performance."


SLIDE 12: APPLICATION SCENARIOS (1.5 minutes)
-------------------------------
"Three scenarios:

Scenario 1 - Environmental Awareness: YOLOv8, target <1000ms, achieved 850ms. Use: object identification for stationary users.

Scenario 2 - Text Reading: Tesseract OCR, target <1500ms, achieved 1240ms. Use: documents, signage, labels.

Scenario 3 - Social Interaction: Face recognition, target <1000ms, achieved 980ms. Use: identifying known individuals.

Suitable for: stationary/slow-moving users, environmental scanning, text reading, social assistance.

Current limitation: high-speed navigation needs <500ms. Hardware acceleration will enable 200-400ms latencies."


SLIDE 13: CONCLUSIONS (1.5 minutes)
-----------------------------------------
"Five achievements:

Privacy-first: 9.8/10 score, complete offline operation.
Multimodal integration: first 4+ modalities on Raspberry Pi 5.
Novel optimizations: 23-31% latency reduction.
Practical viability: sub-$200 hardware.
Robust: zero crashes in 50 hours.

Limitations: navigation constraint, environmental sensitivity, English only.

Future work: hardware acceleration (3-5× speedup), enhanced preprocessing, multi-language support, field evaluation with users.

Extended analysis available in our Sensors journal publication."


SLIDE 14: IMPACT & DEPLOYMENT (1 minute)
-------------------------------------
"Broader impact:

Data sovereignty for vulnerable populations.
Economic accessibility at sub-$200 cost.
Network independence for developing regions.
Open-source enables cultural adaptation.

Responsible deployment requires: user education about capabilities, safety guidelines, secure update mechanisms, community collaboration.

Our framework proves privacy-preserving multimodal AI is achievable on consumer edge hardware."
